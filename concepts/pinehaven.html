A small cabin retreat, tucked into the pines

Slow down.
The forest is in no hurry.

Pinehaven is a quiet cluster of hand finished cabins at the end of a gravel lane, where mornings start with woodsmoke and strong coffee, and evenings end under a sky full of stars.

Some places ask you to do more. Pinehaven asks for less. Fewer plans, slower breakfasts, longer walks, and one good book you actually finish.

The retreat sits on a gentle slope above a creek, surrounded by tall pines that keep the air cool even in high summer. Each cabin faces the trees, not its neighbours, so your porch feels like your own small clearing.

Days here are simple on purpose. Walk the loop trail before lunch, soak in the cedar tub as the light fades, then cook something unhurried in a kitchen that is small but properly equipped.

It is not a resort, and that is the point. It is a place to rest well, eat simply, and hear yourself think again.

Location and getting here

Close enough to reach, far enough to matter.

Pinehaven sits at the end of a maintained gravel lane in pine country, the kind of drive where the signal fades about ten minutes before you arrive, and nobody minds.

The nearest village has a bakery, a small grocery, and a fuel station. Stock up there, because once you are settled on the porch, you will not want to leave for supplies.

In winter, the lane is ploughed but a vehicle with good clearance is a wise idea. In every other season, any ordinary car manages it comfortably.

By car

About two hours from the city on the valley highway, then twenty minutes of winding pine roads to our lane.

By train

The nearest station is in the village. Let us know your arrival time and we can arrange a lift for the last stretch.

By bus

Coaches stop at the village square twice a day. The walk from there is lovely but long, so a pickup is the better plan.

On arrival

Park by the barn, follow the lanterns to your cabin, and find the kettle already filled. That part is tradition.
